\section{Task 3: Optimal Operational Decision-Making Framework for Water Treatment Plants}
\subsection{Task Rationale and Core Modeling Approach}
Task 3 serves as the decision-making phase of the "prediction-decision" closed-loop framework, building directly on the contaminant concentration forecasts derived in Task 2. The primary objective is to generate an optimal 24-hour shutdown/operation schedule for the water treatment plant, minimizing a holistic weighted cost function that balances operational stability, water supply reliability, and public health risks.

To address the temporal dependency of hourly decisions (where each choice impacts future states and costs), a two-tier modeling strategy is adopted:
\begin{itemize}[noitemsep, topsep=0pt]
    \item A \textbf{weighted multi-objective optimization model} to quantify trade-offs across competing cost dimensions.
    \item A \textbf{dynamic programming (DP) model} to solve the time-dependent optimal control problem, ensuring globally optimal decisions over the 24-hour horizon.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Weighted Multi-Objective Cost Function}
The core of the model is a weighted sum of three orthogonal cost components, designed to reflect the relative priority of operational stability, supply reliability, and public health:
\begin{equation}
\min \quad \text{Total Cost} = \alpha \cdot C_{\text{shutdown}} + \beta \cdot C_{\text{supply}} + \gamma \cdot C_{\text{risk}}
\label{eq:total_cost}
\end{equation}
where normalized weights $\alpha=0.35$, $\beta=0.25$, and $\gamma=0.40$ prioritize risk mitigation, followed by operational cost and supply reliability.

\subsubsection{Shutdown Cost}
This component captures direct operational expenses incurred during plant deactivation:
\begin{equation}
C_{\text{shutdown}} = \sum_{t=0}^{23} C_s \cdot U(t)
\label{eq:shutdown_cost}
\end{equation}
- $U(t) \in \{0,1\}$: Binary decision variable (1 = shutdown, 0 = operation) at time $t$.
- $C_s$: Hourly cost of plant shutdown (defined in the problem statement).
- The summation accumulates costs only for periods when the plant is shut down, aligning with real-world operational economics.

\subsubsection{Supply Loss Cost}
This component quantifies the economic and societal impact of unmet water demand:
\begin{equation}
C_{\text{supply}} = \sum_{t=0}^{23} C_l \cdot \max\left(D(t) - Q(t) \cdot (1-U(t)), \, 0\right)
\label{eq:supply_cost}
\end{equation}
- $D(t)$/$Q(t)$: Water demand/supply rate at time $t$ (in $\text{m}^3$).
- $\max(\cdot,0)$: Ensures only positive supply deficits are penalized (avoids non-physical negative costs).
- $C_l$: Unit cost of supply loss (reflecting the value of unmet residential/industrial demand).

\subsubsection{Risk Cost (Concentration-Dependent Penalty)}
This component incorporates health risk penalties tied to contaminant concentrations, operationalizing the trade-off between water supply and public safety:
\begin{equation}
C_{\text{risk}} = \sum_{t=0}^{23} \left[ k \cdot \max\left(C(t) - C_{\text{safe}}, \, 0\right) \right] \cdot Q(t) \cdot (1-U(t))
\label{eq:risk_cost}
\end{equation}
- $C(t)$: Contaminant concentration (from Task 2 forecasts, in mg/L); $C_{\text{safe}}$: Regulatory safety threshold.
- $k \cdot \max\left(C(t) - C_{\text{safe}}, \, 0\right)$: Linear penalty for concentrations exceeding safe limits (proportional risk pricing).
- $Q(t) \cdot (1-U(t))$: Restricts risk costs to operational periods (no contaminated water supply during shutdowns).

\subsection{Dynamic Programming Formulation}
The DP model solves the time-dependent optimal control problem using Bellman's principle of optimality, decomposing the 24-hour horizon into sequential, solvable subproblems.

\subsubsection{State Definition}
The state vector at time $t$ is defined as $V(t, C(t), S(t), U(t-1))$, representing the minimum total cost from time $t$ to 23, given:
\begin{itemize}[noitemsep, topsep=0pt]
    \item Contaminant concentration $C(t)$ (Task 2 output),
    \item Emergency storage level $S(t)$ (in $\text{m}^3$),
    \item Previous shutdown decision $U(t-1)$ (captures operational hysteresis).
\end{itemize}
This definition ensures the state exhibits the \textbf{Markovian property} (future states depend only on the current state), satisfying DP optimality conditions.

\subsubsection{State Transition Equation}
The Bellman equation for state transition is:
\begin{equation}
V(t, \cdot) = \min_{U(t) \in \{0,1\}} \left[ \text{Instant Cost}(t, U(t)) + V(t+1, \cdot) \right]
\label{eq:dp_transition}
\end{equation}
- $\text{Instant Cost}(t, U(t))$: Sum of the three cost components (Section 2) for decision $U(t)$ at time $t$.
- The $\min$ operator selects the decision (shutdown/operation) that minimizes the sum of immediate and future costs, ensuring global optimality.

\subsubsection{Boundary Condition}
At the end of the 24-hour horizon ($t=24$), no future costs are incurred:
\begin{equation}
V(24, \cdot) = 0
\label{eq:dp_boundary}
\end{equation}
This terminates backward recursion and provides a starting point for DP solution.

\subsubsection{Constraints}
Physical/operational constraints ensure model feasibility:
\begin{enumerate}[label=\arabic*., noitemsep, topsep=0pt]
    \item \textbf{Storage Balance}: Tracks emergency storage evolution:
        \begin{equation}
        S(t+1) = S(t) + Q_{\text{in}}(t) - Q_{\text{out}}(t) \cdot (1-U(t))
        \label{eq:storage_balance}
        \end{equation}
    \item \textbf{Concentration Limit}: Prohibits operation above safe concentrations:
        \begin{equation}
        C(t) \leq C_{\text{max}} \quad (\text{if } U(t)=0)
        \label{eq:conc_limit}
        \end{equation}
    \item \textbf{Non-negativity}: Ensures physical feasibility:
        \begin{equation}
        S(t) \geq 0, \quad Q(t) \geq 0, \quad U(t) \in \{0,1\}
        \label{eq:non_neg}
        \end{equation}
\end{enumerate}
